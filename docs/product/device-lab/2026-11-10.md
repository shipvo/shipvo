### Safety scenarios

- **Tampered bundles**: bad signature rejected before download. Bad hash and bad size rejected after download. The app stayed alive on release 9 each time, restarted on release 9, and a valid release then applied.
- **Hermes bytecode mismatch**: release 13 declared for bytecode 98 on a shell with 99. The device polled the pointer, never fetched the bundle, blocked release 13, stayed on release 9. The refusal reason was in telemetry.
- **Rollback**: the earlier bundle was republished as a new release. The on-device call (`Runtime.rollback()` from the sample app's button) restored release 18, which stayed after a force-stop and relaunch. Release 19 was blocked and not downloaded again.
- **Staged rollout**: published to `b0` and `b1` only. The device (band `b2`, cohort 188 in this run) polled 4 times, downloaded nothing of the new release and stayed on release 18. Widening applied it in 6,100 ms.
- **Crash on start**: in 3 of 3 trials the bad release got 3 boots, was discarded, and the previous release came back.
- **Late crash**: in 3 of 3 trials the bad release ran 3 times and the previous release came back, in 51 to 53 s.
- **Resumed download**: the server cut the first response at 1,010,393 B. The app retried on the next poll with `Range: bytes=1010393-`, got a 206, and the release became healthy.
