The 10.7 s and 11.3 s trials are publishes that landed just after the app's 10 s poll. The 301 B patch is an update whose content was identical to the base. That is why the patch size is the median, 27,209 B.

### Safety scenarios

- **Tampered bundles**: bad signature rejected before any download; bad hash and bad size rejected after download. In all three the app stayed alive, `current_release` stayed 7, `pending_release` was 0 and a relaunch came up on release 7. A valid release then applied.
- **Rollback**: the earlier bundle was republished as a new release. The SHA-256 of the on-device bundle matched the target in 3 of 3 trials. The on-device `Runtime.rollback()` call is not driven.
- **Staged rollout**: published to `b0` and `b2` only. The device (in `b1`) polled 4 times, downloaded nothing of the new release and stayed on release 14. Widening applied it in 6,340 ms.
- **Crash on start**: in every trial the bad release got 3 boots (2 bad runs counted before the revert), was discarded and blocked, `crash_loop_revert` was reported, release 15 came back and the bad bundle was not downloaded again.
- **Late crash**: in all 3 trials the bad release was blocked, `crash_loop_revert` was reported, release 15 came back and the bundle was not downloaded again. Trial 1 was promoted without a pending trial stage (`staged: false` in the JSON), so it took 4 launches with 3 runs of the bad release and restored 128,187 ms after publish. Trials 2 and 3 went through the trial-boot path (`staged: true`), 3 launches each, restored in 51,920 and 53,699 ms. The median restore time of 53.7 s therefore mixes two paths; trial 1 is the like-for-like number for the "late crash" row in the 2026-11-05 report from 65d91df (128 s). Both are within the limit of 3 runs of the bad release.

On the same CPU type, everything is within 7% except two numbers: patch download (+17%, under the 20% line) and patch pointer to healthy (+22%).

The patch pointer-to-healthy number is the one over the line. Its three trials are 359, 294 and 10,694 ms. The 359 ms trial is the 301 B patch (identical content), the 294 ms trial is the same as 2026-11-04's 294 ms median, and the third missed the poll. With only 3 trials the median is just the first trial. The earlier 2026-11-05 run (EPYC 9V45) also read +27% on this measure (374 ms) and was explained away as noise there. Two runs on two host types over the line is enough that I filed an issue rather than explain it away again.

### With the earlier 2026-11-05 run (commit 65d91df, EPYC 9V45)

That run was 19% to 52% faster on cold start, apply and download than 2026-11-04. This run, on the EPYC 7763 again, is back to the 2026-11-04 level. So the large swings on 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-05 follow the host CPU type, which fits the reading in #103. It does not prove it: #103 was about an Intel Xeon 8370C host, which I have not measured.
