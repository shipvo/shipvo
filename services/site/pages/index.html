How it works

Four stages, one codebase. Click through; every panel is the real thing, not a mock.

A complete Expo app in one command.

shipvo init writes the OTA runtime, the device API, a simulator build, a publish workflow and your signing key. Your screens use one API for camera, location, biometrics, payments and notifications; it is real on phones and simulated for agents, and never pretends otherwise.

Build your first app →
$ npx -y @shipvo/cli init myshop --name "My Shop"
✓ shipvo.json · app.json · App.tsx · metro.config.js
✓ .github/workflows/publish.yml
✓ .shipvo/seed.txt  (Ed25519 signing key, git-ignored)

Ask Claude or ChatGPT to use it.

One line of MCP config. The agent gets each screen as an accessibility tree with refs, then taps, types and scrolls. Permission dialogs, the fingerprint prompt and payment sheets appear in the tree and it answers them like a person. end_session reports what your backend actually received.

Push to main. Every phone has it.

The workflow signs a release pointer (Ed25519), ships bsdiff patches from each installed version, and rolls out by cohort: 1 %, then 10 %, then everyone. Phones verify signature, hash and size, apply without a restart, and discard a release that fails to boot. shipvo rollback --to 7 when you need it.

What the simulator is notIt is not a real device. Payments, biometrics and the camera are simulated and say so. Use it for most of your testing, and a phone for the last mile.
