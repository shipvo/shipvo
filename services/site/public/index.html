Three things, each measured.

Shipvo does not ask you to trust it. Every claim below links to a number taken on real hardware and published in the repo.

01 · PUBLISH

Push to main. Every phone has it.

Ed25519-signed release pointers, SHA-256-verified bundles, bsdiff patches from each installed version, staged cohorts (1 % → 10 % → everyone) and a one-command rollback. The shell pins your public key; nothing delivered over the air can change it.

Typical update tens of KB · applied on a 2 GB Redmi 9A without a restart
02 · SIMULATE

Agents use your app like a person does.

Over MCP an agent gets each screen as an accessibility tree with refs, then taps, types and scrolls. Camera, location, fingerprint, payments and notifications are simulated and clearly labelled so. Local with one command, or hosted with a key.

Works in Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Claude Code · ChatGPT placed a full order in 18 steps, 76 s
03 · VERIFY

Simulator vs real device, scored nightly.

The same scripted flows run in the simulator, on an Android emulator and on an iPhone simulator. We compare every actionable element and every text, and publish the gaps, not just the score.

Actionable parity 100 % on 3 flows, both platforms · text 98.6 % / 96.2 %

From nothing to an agent-tested, OTA-updated app.

About twenty minutes. You need Node 22. No Android Studio, no Xcode, no phone, until you want one.

  1. Create the app

    shipvo init writes a complete Expo app: the OTA runtime wired in, the device API, a simulator build, a publish workflow and your signing key.

  2. Let an agent use it

    npm run sim serves it. Add one line of MCP config and ask Claude or ChatGPT to open it, scan a barcode, pay, and tell you what broke.

  3. Register and publish

    shipvo login, shipvo app create, then push. The workflow signs the release and every phone and simulator gets it. Open myshop@latest to see exactly what phones run.

  4. List it, if you want

    Flip listing.public in shipvo.json and your app appears on shipvo.dev/apps, where any signed-in agent can open it.

What the simulator is notIt is not a real device. Payments, biometrics and the camera are simulated and say so (SIMULATED- receipts, simulated: true in every log line). Use it for most of your testing, and a phone for the last mile.
What agents do todayThey test and demonstrate your app. They do not yet buy things for real people through it: that needs OAuth consent, spend limits and a checkout contract, which is on the roadmap, not in the product.
